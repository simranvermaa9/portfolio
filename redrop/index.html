01

THE PROBLEM

MANAGING PCOS SHOULDN'T
FEEL THIS OVERWHELMING.

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome is one of the most common hormonal disorders among women of reproductive age — bringing irregular cycles, excess androgen, acne, weight fluctuations, and long-term health risks.

Despite how common it is, managing it feels fragmented. Information is scattered. Doctor visits are infrequent. Lifestyle changes are hard to sustain. Most women navigate it alone.

02

DESIGN PROCESS

DESIGNING WITH USERS,
NOT ON ASSUMPTIONS.

Redrop followed a User-Centered Design approach — an iterative process keeping real needs central at every stage. Before building a single feature, I needed to know exactly who this was for.

The scope widened beyond tracking — the product had to educate, guide, and assist, not just monitor.
01

THE NEWLY WORRIED

Where do I start?

THE DIAGNOSED

THE SUPPORTERS

03

QUALITATIVE RESEARCH

LISTENING
BEFORE DESIGNING.

I ran six in-depth interviews to understand lived experiences with PCOS — chasing depth over volume. The conversations surfaced three patterns again and again: inconsistent follow-ups, confusion around treatment, and difficulty sustaining lifestyle change.

WHAT I ASKED

  1. How long have you been diagnosed with PCOD / PCOS?
  2. What kind of treatment do you prefer, and why?
  3. How often do you actually visit your doctor?
  4. What does your daily management routine look like?
“I get told what to do, but never why — so the moment it gets hard, I just stop.”Recurring sentiment across interviews

09

HI-FIDELITY

THE SHIPPED
DESIGN.

The final screens brought the system together — onboarding, a cycle tracker, symptom logging, doctor discovery, and appointment booking — in one warm, approachable interface designed to make a clinical topic feel manageable, not medical.

10

REFLECTION

WHAT ITOOK AWAY.

01

Trust is a design problem

Half of surveyed users were hesitant to trust an app with medical data. That wasn't a reason to drop features — it was a brief to design transparency, control, and reassurance into every sensitive flow.

02

Scope beyond the obvious

Designing for carers and the newly-suspecting — not just the diagnosed — reframed Redrop from a tracker into an ecosystem that educates and guides.

03

Depth over volume works

Six honest interviews surfaced more usable insight than the survey alone could. The qualitative depth is what made the persona and flows feel real.
