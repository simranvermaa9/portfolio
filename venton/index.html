THE BRIEF

MAKING EMOTIONAL TOOLS
FEEL HUMAN.

Vent-On is a Gen-Z mental-health companion designed to help users track emotions, recognise patterns, and build habits. But the core idea wasn't the features — it was the tone.

I was the first and only designer, owning product design, user flow, design system, brand language, and hi-fi prototyping end to end.

The question behind the project: can a mental-health app have a personality without trivialising mental health?
02

THE PROBLEM

MOST MENTAL-HEALTH APPS
DON'T FEEL HUMAN.

Clean, minimal, well-structured — yet the experience felt sterile. The tone was clinical, interfaces were rigid, and interactions resembled filling out a report more than having a conversation.

01

Clinical tone

The products spoke like institutions when users needed something warm and approachable.

02

Rigid interaction

Logging emotions felt like completing a form rather than checking in with yourself.

03

Low return value

People still needed support, but the experience gave them little reason to come back.

03

RESEARCH

PEOPLE AREN'T AVOIDING SUPPORT.
THEY'RE AVOIDING HOW IT FEELS.

I ran a survey and six interviews about people's relationship with mental-health apps. The responses weren't surprising — but they were telling.

  • They struggle with anxiety, overwhelm, or inconsistent routines.
  • They have tried mental-health apps but stopped returning.
  • They drop off not from lack of need, but because the app doesn't feel relatable enough.
“A lot of times I feel lonely but the apps don't help.”
“The tone of these apps is so depressing!”
“Are we sure the copywriters are not depressed?”
04

AFFINITY MAPPING

THE QUESTIONS BECAME CLEARER
BEFORE THE ANSWERS DID.

After surveys and interviews, I translated recurring concerns into “How Might We” questions. They naturally grouped around two emotional territories.

ISOLATION

Help someone feel less alone.

  • How might we help users feel less isolated using mindfulness?
  • How might we prevent social isolation in their lives?
  • How might we motivate them to get out of their isolation?

ALLEVIATING

Make the moment feel lighter.

  • How might we alleviate anxiety with social interaction?
  • How might we help users take their mind off the problem?
  • How might we help them train their mind to dedramatise problems?

06

THE CONCEPT

WHAT IF YOUR MENTAL-HEALTH APP
HAD A PERSONALITY?

A Gen-Z-friendly companion that helps users track emotions and habits — but with character. Not comedic, not trivial. Just approachable. The features other apps have too; the tone is what makes someone stay.

Clinical→Approachable

Warm, expressive, and human where other apps feel sterile and institutional.

Instructional→Conversational

It talks with you rather than at you — closer to a check-in than a form.

Performance→Check-in

No streaks to defend or scores to chase. Low-stakes and judgment-free.

07

MOODBOARDING

DESIGNING THE EMOTIONAL LAYER
FIRST.

Since the app targets people who typically avoid self-help apps for being too corny, I built it in the style of Gen Z — reassuring, modern, and gentle. Neobrutalism brought solid borders, cutouts, bold type, and expressive illustrations.

DESIGN DECISIONS

THE CHOICES
THAT DEFINED IT.

INSTEAD OF

Clinical, minimal UI

Clean grids, muted tones, and serious typography — the visual language used by every major mental-health app.

I CHOSE

Neobrutalism

Bold borders, warm cream backgrounds, and expressive illustrations. A system that feels like a friend, not a therapist's office.

INSTEAD OF

Reflection as performance

Emotion tracking as a task — streaks, scores, and progress metrics.

I CHOSE

Reflection as check-in

Low-stakes conversation. Meme-worthy image options. No right answers and no judgment.

INSTEAD OF

Feature coverage first

Packing in every wellness feature before deciding what the product should feel like.

I CHOSE

Personality first, features second

MoSCoW made tone a must-have. Three core flows done with care beat fifteen flows done with none.

12

REFLECTION

WHAT THISTAUGHT ME.

01

Tone is a feature

I used to think voice and personality were the last 5% of a product. Vent-On taught me they're the first decision. Everything else follows.

02

Start with the emotional layer

Moodboarding before wireframing changed how I approach consumer products. Getting the feeling right before the layout saved weeks of rework.

03

Neobrutalism is a decision, not a trend

Choosing a visual language because it communicates something — not because it's in style — is the difference between a designed system and a styled one.
